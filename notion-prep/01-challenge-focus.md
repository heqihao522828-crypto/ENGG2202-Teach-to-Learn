# Gate 01 · Challenge Focus

> **Decision for this Gate**  
> Which Green Technology challenge direction is worth investigating next, and
> what evidence could make your team change direction?

## What this Gate is for

A technology is not yet a problem. “We want to use AI,” “we want to build a
robot” or “we want to make a solar device” describes an interest or possible
response—not a validated engineering challenge.

At this Gate, your team will explore several directions before selecting one
promising challenge to investigate. You are not expected to know the final
solution yet.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of Gate 01, your team should be able to:

1. connect a Green Technology interest to one or more relevant SDG targets;
2. describe a real context rather than a technology label;
3. identify possible stakeholders and people whose decisions may be affected;
4. separate early evidence from assumptions;
5. justify why one direction deserves further investigation;
6. name the evidence that could cause the team to change direction.

## Start here

### 1 · Form the project team

Discuss interests, disciplines, practical experience and skills the team wants
to develop. A useful team does not need identical interests or existing skills.
It needs a workable way to make decisions and share responsibility.

Keep:

- team member names and roles for this Gate;
- what each person hopes to learn;
- existing strengths and important gaps;
- how the team will communicate and make decisions.

### 2 · Explore challenge sources

Look for challenges before choosing a solution. Possible starting points include:

- the [UN Sustainable Development Goals](https://sdgs.un.org/goals);
- local campus, community or environmental observations;
- World Engineering Day Hackathon challenge themes;
- stakeholder conversations;
- existing research, open-source projects and public datasets;
- problems the team has personally experienced or repeatedly observed.

For each possible direction, record:

- the context or place;
- who may care about it;
- what currently happens;
- why the situation might matter;
- what is known from a source or observation;
- what is still only an assumption.

### 3 · Compare at least three directions

Do not select a project only because it is familiar or looks easy to build.
Compare at least three directions using questions such as:

- Is there a real and reachable context?
- Can we identify a possible stakeholder?
- Is there a decision, outcome or practice that could improve?
- Can the team gather useful evidence within the course?
- Is the challenge meaningful but still small enough to investigate?
- What could the team learn by working on it?
- Is an engineering response relevant, or would a non-technical response be better?

### 4 · Select a provisional challenge focus

Choose one direction to take into Gate 02. This is a provisional decision, not
a permanent commitment. A strong team changes direction when the evidence no
longer supports the original idea.

Use this sentence structure:

> We are investigating **[context / challenge]** for **[possible stakeholder]**
> because **[initial evidence or observation]** suggests that **[decision,
> outcome or practice]** may need improvement. We still need to validate
> **[important assumptions]**.

## Green Technology and SDG check

Use an SDG as a lens, not as decoration.

Record:

- relevant goal number and name;
- the specific target, if identifiable;
- why the target is connected to the context;
- what environmental, social or resource trade-off may need investigation;
- which sustainability claim would be too early to make.

## Suggested tools and learning resources

| Need | Possible support | How to use it responsibly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explore SDG themes | UN SDG goals and targets | Read the target; do not choose from the icon alone. |
| Search current information | Google Scholar, Perplexity, Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude, DeepSeek or Kimi | Follow sources, compare claims and keep a search log. |
| Organise team thinking | Miro, FigJam, Canva Whiteboard or paper | Keep alternatives and disagreements visible. |
| Track project work | GitHub Issues / Project board | Record decisions and owners, not only a task list. |
| Create the checkpoint | Provided Gate 01 template | Replace prompts with concise evidence and links. |

AI may help generate search terms, summarise sources or challenge an assumption.
It does not own the final project decision. Your team remains responsible for
checking sources, identifying uncertainty and explaining the choice.

## Evidence to keep

Your team repository should preserve:

- team and working agreement;
- at least three challenge directions considered;
- source links, observations or early stakeholder clues;
- an assumption log;
- the provisional challenge focus statement;
- relevant SDG target(s) and justification;
- the reason other directions were not selected;
- questions for Gate 02;
- a short reflection on how the team’s thinking changed.

## Tuesday Studio discussion

Bring evidence, not only an idea. Be ready to discuss:

1. Why is this direction worth investigating?
2. Which statement is supported by evidence?
3. Which statement is still an assumption?
4. Who might be the primary stakeholder?
5. What evidence would make the team change direction?
6. What obstacle or knowledge gap needs instructor / TA support?

## Gate 01 checkpoint

Complete the provided checkpoint template and submit it through the official
course channel announced by the instructor. Formal dates, marks and submission
requirements belong in Moodle / Ed Discussion, not on this page.

### Ready to move to Gate 02 when

- the team has considered alternatives;
- one provisional direction is clearly stated;
- the context and possible stakeholder are visible;
- evidence and assumptions are separated;
- relevant SDG target(s) are explained;
- the team knows what must be validated next.
